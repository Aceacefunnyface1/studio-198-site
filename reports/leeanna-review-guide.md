# Leeanna Review Guide

This guide captures Leeanna's review style based on the current site data. Her voice is craft-first, structured, and focused on execution over hype. She tends to evaluate atmosphere, pacing, control, escalation, and payoff, then closes with a clear tier judgment.

## Core Voice

- Lead with craft, not fandom.
- Focus on execution: pacing, structure, tension, visual logic, atmosphere, and payoff.
- Call out weaknesses as flaws of control, drag, repetition, or thin characterization.
- Use clean, direct sentences rather than slang-heavy reactions.
- End by clearly placing the movie in a tier.

## Short Template

```md
## Quick Hit
[Movie Title] is a [genre/era] film that works best when its [atmosphere / pacing / structure / tension] locks in. [One short sentence about whether it lands or slips.]

## Full Take
From a craft standpoint, the film is strongest when its [visual design / structure / mood] stays aligned. It earns points for [clarity / tension / atmosphere / control] but loses some on [drag / repetition / thin characterization / uneven payoff]. The concept is only as strong as the clarity of its escalation. Final result: [ELITE / SOLID / BAD / TRASH].
```

## Longer Polished Template

```md

## Full Take
From a craft standpoint, the film is strongest when its [design / pacing / visual logic / structure / atmosphere] stays aligned. The movie earns points for [clarity of intent / escalation / mood / tension / formal control], and those strengths usually matter more than the rougher edges. Its weaknesses are usually [repetition / drag / thin characterization / uneven rhythm / weak payoff] rather than total failure of ambition.

What gives the film its identity is [the specific thing that makes it feel distinct: period atmosphere, creature mechanics, psychological pressure, gothic imagery, emotional undercurrent, etc.]. When that element is handled with control, the movie holds together. When it slips, the experience feels thinner than it should. The concept is only as strong as the clarity of its escalation. Overall, that places it in [ELITE / SOLID / BAD / TRASH].
```

## Rating Tier Examples

### ELITE

Use when the movie is controlled, memorable, and pays off what it sets up.

Example language:

- "[Movie Title] is a [genre/era] piece whose value comes down to control, atmosphere, and payoff. It knows exactly what it wants to do and has the discipline to land it."
- "From a craft standpoint, the film is strongest when its pacing and design stay aligned. The atmosphere does real work here, and the escalation keeps tightening without wasting energy."
- "What gives the film its identity is the confidence of its visual logic. Even when the technique shows its age, the control is visible."
- "Overall, that places it in ELITE."

### SOLID

Use when the movie works more than it fails, even if it has visible weaknesses.

Example language:

- "[Movie Title] shows clear design choices, even when the material itself is a little uneven."
- "The film earns points for clarity and mood, then loses some on repetition and pacing drag."
- "Its strengths matter more than its rougher edges, which is why the movie still lands."
- "Overall, that places it in SOLID."

### BAD

Use when the movie has some craft value but the weaknesses keep undercutting it.

Example language:

- "[Movie Title] has the outline of something stronger, but the execution never fully tightens."
- "From a craft standpoint, the film is strongest when its mood and design stay aligned, but too much of it slips into repetition or thin characterization."
- "There are ideas here, but the payoff is weaker than the setup."
- "Net result: BAD."

### TRASH

Use when the movie fails at both execution and payoff, or collapses under its own ideas.

Example language:

- "[Movie Title] never really develops the control or clarity needed to make its premise work."
- "The pacing drags, the escalation is muddy, and the film's choices never build toward anything satisfying."
- "Whatever value the concept has gets buried under weak execution."
- "Final result: TRASH."
